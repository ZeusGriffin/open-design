## Purpose

This is a reference point for future original 3D-print builds. Do not clone the referenced object. Study the underlying design logic, fabrication process, connectors, assembly strategy, proportions, and how separate parts form a cohesive physical system.

## Design philosophy to carry forward

- Design as a system of compatible parts rather than a single fixed object.
- Favor modular, replaceable, printable components.
- Use repeated geometry, alignment, and connector language so parts visually and mechanically belong together.
- Hide complexity where possible; keep the exterior clean.
- Prefer tool-light assembly, snap fits, slots, rails, tabs, magnets, captured hardware, or other simple repeatable connectors where structurally appropriate.
- Separate structural parts from cosmetic shells so either can evolve without rebuilding everything.
- Design around real manufacturing constraints: printer bed, orientation, tolerances, supports, wall thickness, fasteners, wiring, and service access.
- Preserve a clear path from concept → dimensioned CAD → prototype → fit test → revised printable model.

## Future-build extraction checklist

When this reference is used, extract:
1. Overall construction sequence.
2. How parts locate and align.
3. Connector/joint types.
4. Load-bearing versus cosmetic geometry.
5. Print orientation and likely support strategy.
6. Tolerance/clearance assumptions.
7. Modular or replaceable elements.
8. Cable/electronics routing where relevant.
9. Assembly/disassembly path.
10. Ideas that can be translated into an original BLINK / 925 Studios design.

## Cohesion rule

Future builds should reuse a consistent connector vocabulary and dimensional logic whenever practical. A new project may borrow the *process and principles* documented here, but its geometry and product identity should remain original.
